macro librarywork/libs/Equations/src/Equations.xtlm

Equations: its macro library -- e_q<, t_ex< and b_oth<: an equation as printed in a paper, to its value, its LaTeX, or both. Imported with the functions, under one alias: "e:" u_se< "Equations". A macro is a function from the source text written left and right of its call to the source that replaces the call, run before the program is compiled (X_eTaL MC10). Names with m: are macros; those under h: are private to this file.

source · imports t: work/libs/Strings/src/Strings.xtl; pa: work/libs/Parsing/src/Parsing.xtl

The macros

ᵐe̲q< : Char -> Char -> Char

macro · line 31

The value of an equation written as a paper prints it. The left names the result, its free indices and their extents ("Y[o,h,w] | O Ho Wo", or "L[] | " for a scalar). The right is an expression over the free indices in which a parenthesized sum is a term: "(sum j:=0..M-1 | W[i,j] * x[j]) + b[i]" A sum is an operator word (sum, prod, max, min), an index, := and an inclusive range low..high, then | and its body; several, separated by commas, nest outermost first. A right side that is one bare sum needs no parentheses: "sum c:=0..C-1, sum r:=0..Kh-1 | X[c,h+r] * W[c,r]". Expressions follow math precedence: ^ binds tightest (right to left), then * and /, then + and - (left to right); parentheses group; the functions are exp(x), log(x), sqrt(x), abs(x), max(a, b) and min(a, b); A[e1,e2] reads one element of the caller's array A, one index per axis, from 0. Every reading rule uses all of its text or refuses the call, and what was read is written back and compared with the text at every call, so nothing written is ever dropped.

ᵐe̲q< ← { lhs rhs → (ʰf̲ree lhs) ʰv̲alue ʰc̲hecked rhs }
