Equations: its macro library -- e_q<, t_ex< and b_oth<: an equation as printed in a paper, to its value, its LaTeX, or both. Imported with the functions, under one alias: "e:" u_se< "Equations". ᵐe̲q< : Char -> Char -> Char

macro · line 46

The value of an equation written as a paper prints it. The left names the result, its free indices and their extents ("Y[o,h,w] | O Ho Wo", or "L[] | " for a scalar). The right is an expression over the free indices in which a parenthesized sum is a term: "(sum j:=0..M-1 | W[i,j] * x[j]) + b[i]" A sum is an operator word (sum, prod, max, min), an index, := and an inclusive range low..high, then | and its body; several, separated by commas, nest outermost first. A right side that is one bare sum needs no parentheses: "sum c:=0..C-1, sum r:=0..Kh-1 | X[c,h+r] * W[c,r]". The word integral binds a continuous variable over Float bounds and takes the value of its body by a quadrature rule written after by: "integral tau:=0..1 by simpson 64 | exp(tau)" (trapezoid or simpson, then the number of steps; without by, trapezoid 64; simpson needs an even number). The variable is the grid of n + 1 points a, a + h, ..., b; a bound of inf is refused (write a truncation and show the tail is small, or change variables). Trapezoid is exact on lines, simpson on cubics; at a jump of the body the sample takes the body's value at that point as written. Expressions follow math precedence: ^ binds tightest (right to left), then * and /, then + and - (left to right); parentheses group; the functions are exp(x), log(x), sqrt(x), abs(x), sin(x), cos(x), max(a, b) and min(a, b); A[e1,e2] reads one element of the caller's array A, one index per axis, from 0. Every reading rule uses all of its text or refuses the call, and what was read is written back and compared with the text at every call, so nothing written is ever dropped. Both sides are notations (X_eTaL MC32): where a program is drawn (the pages, the live demo, xetal render --html) the call's two strings are shown as one math box, what e:t_ex< gives for them, after the macro's name; the value and the text are unchanged. draws: t_ex<

ᵐc̲onvolve< : Char -> Char -> Char

macro · line 80

Discrete 1-D convolution and cross-correlation, written as the sums they are (X_eTaL Saga 42 decision 5: library macros over sum, not words of the grammar). The left names the result, its index and the boundary mode, "y[n] | full"; the right names the signal and the kernel, two of the caller's vectors, "x h". The modes: full gives L + M - 1 values (every overlap, the signal padded with M - 1 zeros at each end), same the L centered on full (as numpy centers them), valid the L - M + 1 complete overlaps (a kernel longer than the signal stops the program when it runs); no mode is implicit. Convolution is y[n] = sum over m of x[n - m] h[m], the kernel reversed as it slides; correlation y[n] = sum over m of x[n + m] h[m], not reversed, so correlation is convolution with the kernel reversed. xetal expand shows the sum each call becomes. Both sides are notations (MC32), drawn by e:c_onvolveTex<. draws: c_onvolveTex<
